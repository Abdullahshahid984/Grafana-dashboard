Hi team,

Sharing the findings from the latency investigation on api-rewards-v1 for Monday Nov 2, 18:30-20:30 UTC (13:30-15:30 ET). Monday Oct 26 at the same local time was used as the baseline.

**Summary**

APIM, Protegrity and Fiserv performed normally during the window. The latency increase was in backend time on the entry rewards APIs, not in APIM gateway time. On the api-rewards-v1 side, the three pods that had been running since Oct 1 used about 4x the CPU per request of the younger pod and responded nearly twice as slowly, and their CPU had risen every week through October. Two scale-out pods were added during the window, and both were gone about 6 minutes later after failing readiness with HTTP 500, the same failure seen on an existing pod on Oct 26. The deployment was updated on Nov 3 and current CPU per pod is low.

**What we ruled out**

|---|---|---|---|
| APIM gateway (rewards APIs on prd-001, prd-ext-apim-001, prd-partner-001) | Gateway time p95 | 1-44 ms | 1-44 ms |
| APIM partner instance | Capacity avg / peak | - | ~32% / 64% |

**Recommended actions**

1. Investigate why CPU and memory per pod grow with uptime. The app's Micrometer metrics in the Azure Monitor workspace amw-bfhaks-ihub-cus-prd-01 cover Oct 1 to Nov 3, including JVM heap, GC and thread metrics. A heap dump or profile from a pod that's two to three weeks old would also help.

2. Investigate why pods return 500 on readiness. This happened on new pods during scale-out on Nov 2 and on an existing pod on Oct 26. Worth checking which health indicators are part of the readiness group.

3. Adjust the scaling config. Lower the KEDA Prometheus threshold to match this service's real capacity, something in the range of 15-20 rps per pod as a starting point, and raise minReplicaCount from 4 to 6.
