> **APIM and Traceable: findings (item 1)**
>
> **Timeline (UTC, 6 Nov):** the Traceable named value was created at 04:21 and the policy written at 04:23 and 04:24. A different account changed the policy again at 17:33 (the rollback). It was live for about 13 hours.
>
> **How the integration works:** the Traceable policy sends each request to the Traceable agent and, in the standard (synchronous) version, [waits for its reply before sending the call to the backend](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/data-collection/gateways-load-balancers/azure/azure-apim#:~:text=waits%20for%20a%20response%20from%20ext_cap%20before%20it%20forwards%20the%20request%20to%20the%20backend). APIM finds the agent through the [`traceable-endpoint` named value](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/data-collection/gateways-load-balancers/azure/azure-apim#:~:text=Create%20a%20named%20variable%20traceable-endpoint) on [port 5442](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/installation/optional-installation-topics/agent-ports#:~:text=Traceable%20Module%20Extension) (HTTP) or [5443](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/installation/optional-installation-topics/agent-ports#:~:text=TLS%20Server) (TLS). Our named value points to the agent's internal load balancer, in the correct format.
>
> **Did it work on the APIM side? Yes.** Application Insights recorded about 50 million calls from APIM to the agent over those 13 hours (up to 6.5 million an hour at peak, about 30% of gateway requests) with **no failed calls**, so the network path, including any firewall, was open and the agent answered. (Microsoft [records `send-request` calls as dependency telemetry](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/azure-integration-services-blog/introducing-dependency-telemetry-in-application-insights-for/ba-p/4545504).) Call duration isn't recorded in that telemetry.
>
> **Performance impact: small.** Time inside APIM rose by about 16 ms at the 95th percentile for ordinary requests (27 ms against 11 ms the day before at the same hours). For payloads over 1 MB the slowest 1% went from 6 ms to 408 ms, consistent with the [vendor policy reading whole request bodies into memory](https://downloads.traceable.ai/install/azure-apim/latest/policy.xml) (around line 19 of the file). There were no mass timeouts, and no gateway error is attributable to the Traceable call.
>
> **The Friday incident:** it began around 14:00 UTC and peaked at 16:00, about 10 hours after the integration went in. Backend time tripled, 5xx errors rose tenfold (about 92% returned by the backends, mostly Statements), callers disconnected 13 times more often (StatementsAPI p95 about 30 seconds), and several credit and identity APIs hit 5-second timeouts in their own policies. The evidence points to slow backends and downstream services, not to Traceable. APIM's own processing stayed at tens of milliseconds. (Microsoft notes that an [instance at capacity gets slow and times out instead of throttling](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/api-management-capacity#:~:text=increased%20latency%2C%20dropped%20connections%2C%20and%20time-out%20errors), which is not what we saw here.)
>
> **Is 60 seconds too long?** Yes for a call made on every request. APIM's [default `send-request` timeout is 60 seconds](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#attributes) and [errors are not ignored by default](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#:~:text=the%20error%20will%20be%20ignored). Traceable's [own policy file already sets 10 seconds and fails open](https://downloads.traceable.ai/install/azure-apim/latest/policy.xml) (lines 44 to 46), and even that is long. A lower value should be agreed. Microsoft's own [example uses 20 seconds](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#example).
>
> **Version note:** the vendor's release notes show policy and blocking changes in the last year ([policy safeguards added](https://developer.harness.io/release-notes/runtime-protection-agent/waap-release-notes/2026/agent-release-notes/traceable-platform-agent#:~:text=Improved%20safeguards%20have%20been%20added%20to%20the%20Azure%20APIM%20policy), [new blocking endpoint for Azure APIM](https://developer.harness.io/release-notes/runtime-protection-agent/waap-release-notes/2026/agent-release-notes/traceable-platform-agent#:~:text=Azure%20APIM%20now%20uses%20the%20new%20blocking%20evaluation%20endpoint), [blocking on by default](https://developer.harness.io/release-notes/runtime-protection-agent/waap-release-notes/2026/agent-release-notes/traceable-platform-agent#:~:text=Blocking%20is%20now%20enabled%20by%20default%20for%20all%20deployments)), so the policy and agent versions should be checked against each other.
>
> **Open items for the information security team and the vendor:** did the agent receive and forward the data on 6 November between 04:24 and 17:33 UTC (it has a [`/health` check](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/installation/optional-installation-topics/platform-agent-health-check#:~:text=HTTP%20Health%20Check) that can be tested), did it evaluate or block anything, which agent version was running, and what explains the one call of about 301 seconds in the 16:00 hour.
>
> **Recommendations:**
> 1. Use a short timeout with fail open, agreed with security, or consider the non-blocking policy.
> 2. Limit body capture for large uploads.
> 3. Roll out through the pipeline with a staged ramp-up, not a single write to all APIs.
> 4. On rollback, remove the policy code first, then delete the named values. The backout steps list them in the other order, and the named value is still there.
> 5. Add alerts on APIM capacity (Microsoft suggests [scaling above 60 to 70% for about 30 minutes](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/api-management-capacity#use-capacity-for-scaling-decisions)), on client connection failures, on errors by policy scope, and on the agent load balancer's health probe.
