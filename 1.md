Subject: api-rewards-v1 latency on Nov 2 - findings and recommended actions

Hi team,

Sharing the findings from the latency investigation on api-rewards-v1 for Monday Nov 2, 18:30-20:30 UTC (13:30-15:30 ET). Monday Oct 26 at the same local time was used as the baseline.

Summary

APIM, Protegrity and Fiserv performed normally during the window. The latency increase was in backend time on the entry rewards APIs, not in APIM gateway time. On the api-rewards-v1 side, the three pods that had been running since Oct 1 used about 4x the CPU per request of the younger pod and responded nearly twice as slowly, and their CPU had risen every week through October. Two scale-out pods were added during the window, and both were gone about 6 minutes later after failing readiness with HTTP 500, the same failure seen on an existing pod on Oct 26. The deployment was updated on Nov 3 and current CPU per pod is low.

What we ruled out

APIM: for the rewards APIs on all three instances in the path (prd-001, prd-ext-apim-001 and prd-partner-001), gateway processing time was between 1 and 44 ms at p95, the same as on Oct 26. Capacity on the partner instance averaged around 32% with a peak of 64%.

Protegrity: the tokenization calls made by the partner APIM product policies ran at 6 ms p95.

Fiserv: backend latency for fiservrewardsplp was 537 ms p95 on Nov 2 vs 594 ms on Oct 26, and fiservrewardsclutch was 760 ms vs 752 ms.

Where the latency showed up

The increase was in backend time on the entry instances. On the internal instance, RewardsAPI went from p50 121 ms to 391 ms and p95 1,299 ms to 1,725 ms. On ext, digitalapirewards p95 went from 931 ms to 1,281 ms and RewardsAPI from 949 ms to 1,190 ms. Traffic through APIM was up roughly 50% compared to Oct 26.

What changed on Nov 3

The deployment was updated on Nov 3 at 04:09 UTC. The only difference between the Nov 2 ReplicaSet and the current one is the image tag (harness-1916 to harness-2152). Resources, env vars and JVM settings are unchanged. The current pods are using around 78m of CPU each at about 45 rps total, with around 0.24 s average response time and around 0.8 GB memory. For comparison, vlfqn used 73m in its first week on the old image.

Autoscaling

The KEDA ScaledObject (apirewards-prometheus-scaler) has min 4 and max 12 replicas with two triggers.

The Prometheus trigger uses sum(rate(http_server_requests_seconds_count[5m])) with a threshold of 400 per pod, so total traffic has to exceed 1,600 rps before a 5th pod is added. Traffic at the pods on Nov 2 was around 52 rps in total. The trigger's lastActiveTime is Jan 31.

The CPU trigger is set to 75% of request. Two pods were added during the window, at 19:08 UTC (vbfdv) and 20:42 UTC (l2fzv). Both took traffic (around 8-12 rps at about 0.25 s response time), then failed the readiness probe with HTTP 500 on /actuator/health/readiness, and both were gone about 6 minutes later.

The same readiness failure also happened on an existing pod. On Oct 26 at 16:46 UTC, 74ml9 (running since at least early October) and another pod, h48j9, both failed the readiness probe with HTTP 500. 74ml9 had no restarts and no OOMKill, and it doesn't appear after that point.

Readiness probe: initialDelay 5s, period 5s, timeout 2s, failureThreshold 6. Startup probe: liveness path, initialDelay 30s, period 10s, failureThreshold 6.

Recommended actions

1. Investigate why CPU and memory per pod grow with uptime. The app's Micrometer metrics in the Azure Monitor workspace amw-bfhaks-ihub-cus-prd-01 cover Oct 1 to Nov 3, including JVM heap, GC and thread metrics. A heap dump or profile from a pod that's two to three weeks old would also help.

2. Investigate why pods return 500 on readiness. This happened on new pods during scale-out on Nov 2 and on an existing pod on Oct 26. Worth checking which health indicators are part of the readiness group.

3. Adjust the scaling config. Lower the KEDA Prometheus threshold to match this service's real capacity, something in the range of 15-20 rps per pod as a starting point, and raise minReplicaCount from 4 to 6.
