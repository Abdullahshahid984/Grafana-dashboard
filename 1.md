

> **Item 1: APIM and Traceable integration, findings**
>
> **Background (UTC, 6 Nov):** the Traceable named value was created at 04:21, and the policy was written at 04:23 and 04:24 (a different account changed the policy again at 17:33, the rollback). The integration was live for about 13 hours. The agent sits at an internal load balancer owned by the information security team, and the named value points to it in the correct format.
>
> **1a. If it didn't appear to be working, why? Where are the logs?**
> - On the APIM side it was working. Application Insights recorded about **50 million calls from APIM to the agent** over the 13 hours (up to 6.5 million an hour at peak, about 30% of gateway requests) with **no failed calls**. Microsoft [records `send-request` calls as dependency telemetry](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/azure-integration-services-blog/introducing-dependency-telemetry-in-application-insights-for/ba-p/4545504).
> - No gateway error is attributable to the Traceable call. The only `send-request` errors came from the API-level policies of other APIs (scope `api`, about 5-second timeouts), not from the global Traceable policy.
> - So "not working" most likely refers to what the Traceable side showed or did (data in its console, blocking, forwarding to its cloud). That part is held by the information security team and the vendor.
> - **Where the logs are:** gateway logs in the Log Analytics workspace `law-ads-cus-core-prd-001` (table `ApiManagementGatewayLogs`), outbound-call telemetry in the same workspace (`AppDependencies`) and in Application Insights `apin-ads-cus-entbusops-prd-apim-001`, request data in Splunk through the Event Hub loggers, and the agent's own logs with the information security team. The policy [waits for the agent's reply before calling the backend](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/data-collection/gateways-load-balancers/azure/azure-apim#:~:text=waits%20for%20a%20response%20from%20ext_cap%20before%20it%20forwards%20the%20request%20to%20the%20backend), and because the vendor's file sets [`ignore-error="true"`](view-source:https://downloads.traceable.ai/install/azure-apim/latest/policy.xml) (line 44), a failing call would not appear as a gateway error, which is why the dependency telemetry is the right place to look.
>
> **1b. How did it impact performance? Is the default timeout (60 s) too long?**
> - **Impact was small.** Time inside APIM rose by about **16 ms at the 95th percentile** for ordinary requests (27 ms against 11 ms the day before at the same hours). For payloads over 1 MB the slowest 1% went from 6 ms to 408 ms, consistent with the [vendor policy reading whole request bodies into memory](https://downloads.traceable.ai/install/azure-apim/latest/policy.xml) (around line 19). There were no mass timeouts.
> - **The Friday incident was not caused by it.** The incident began around 14:00 UTC and peaked at 16:00, about 10 hours after the integration went in. Backend time tripled, 5xx errors rose tenfold (about 92% returned by the backend services, mostly Statements), callers disconnected 13 times more often (StatementsAPI p95 about 30 seconds), and several credit and identity APIs hit 5-second timeouts in their own policies. APIM's own processing stayed at tens of milliseconds.
> - **Yes, 60 seconds is too long** for a call made on every request. APIM's [default `send-request` timeout is 60 seconds](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#attributes) and [errors are not ignored by default](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#:~:text=the%20error%20will%20be%20ignored). Traceable's own policy file already sets [10 seconds and fail open](https://downloads.traceable.ai/install/azure-apim/latest/policy.xml) (lines 44 to 46), and even that is long. Recommendation: a timeout of a few seconds, with fail open, agreed with security, or the non-blocking `policy_async.xml` if blocking isn't needed. Microsoft's own [example uses 20 seconds](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/send-request-policy#example).
>
> **1c. TCP connection from the APIM subnet to the Traceable appliance**
> - The 50 million successful calls show APIM could reach the agent on port 5442 throughout, so the network path, including any firewall, was open. There was no blocking by a firewall.
> - If a direct test is still wanted, the agent has a [`/health` check](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/installation/optional-installation-topics/platform-agent-health-check#:~:text=HTTP%20Health%20Check) on [port 5442](https://developer.harness.io/web-application-and-api-protection-waap-protection-protection/installation/optional-installation-topics/agent-ports#:~:text=Traceable%20Module%20Extension) (HTTP) that APIM can call.
>
> **Follow-up items:**
> - The information security team and the vendor (item 3) to confirm from the agent logs that data was received and forwarded between 04:24 and 17:33 UTC on 6 November, whether anything was evaluated or blocked, the agent version in use against the policy version ([policy safeguards](https://developer.harness.io/release-notes/runtime-protection-agent/waap-release-notes/2026/agent-release-notes/traceable-platform-agent#:~:text=Improved%20safeguards%20have%20been%20added%20to%20the%20Azure%20APIM%20policy), [blocking endpoint change](https://developer.harness.io/release-notes/runtime-protection-agent/waap-release-notes/2026/agent-release-notes/traceable-platform-agent#:~:text=Azure%20APIM%20now%20uses%20the%20new%20blocking%20evaluation%20endpoint)), and the one call of about 301 seconds recorded in the 16:00 hour.
> - Before any re-deployment: use a pipeline with a staged ramp-up, limit body capture for large uploads, and on rollback remove the policy code first and then the named values (the backout steps list them the other way round, and the named value is still in place).
> - For item 2: alerts on APIM capacity (Microsoft suggests [scaling above 60 to 70% for about 30 minutes](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/api-management/api-management-capacity#use-capacity-for-scaling-decisions)), client connection failures, errors by policy scope, and the agent load balancer's health probe.
